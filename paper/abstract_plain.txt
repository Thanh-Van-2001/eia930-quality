Recording Errors in EIA-930 Hourly Demand and Forecast Data and Their Effect on Forecast Evaluation and Annual Peaks

The U.S. Form EIA-930 files give hourly demand and day-ahead forecasts for each Lower-48 balancing authority (BA) and are widely used to evaluate load forecasts. Earlier cleaning targets demand alone; to our knowledge, the effect of errors on forecast evaluation has not been measured across BAs. Under a pre-specified plan we screened both series of 53 BAs in 2016–2026 (4.99 million BA-hours) for non-positive values, gross outliers, spikes, flat runs, copied forecasts and timestamp offsets, compared the screen with EIA's adjusted demand by class, and measured the effect on each BA's annual forecast MAPE and peak. Errors are rare: the median BA has 0.022% anomalous demand and 0.026% anomalous forecast hours, and the forecast's rate exceeded demand's in only 23 of 47 BAs. Screening changed a BA's annual MAPE by more than 10% in 15.8% of BA-years and by at least one percentage point in 12.0%. The reported annual peak differed from EIA's adjusted peak by more than 1% in 17.5% of BA-years, mostly because gross errors more than doubled it. EIA's adjustments are nearly a subset of our flags but leave all flat runs and all but 5% of spikes unchanged, and never touch the forecast. An exploratory monthly scan confirms a recently reported one-hour offset between PJM's forecast and demand since February 2019. PJM's official peak hours place it in the forecast; realignment lowers PJM's MAPE from 3.6% to 2.4%. CISO, AZPS and SC show shorter offsets. Both series should be screened and aligned before any evaluation.

Keywords: Data quality, load forecasting, forecast evaluation, peak demand, EIA-930.
