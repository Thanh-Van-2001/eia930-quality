Recording Errors in EIA-930 Hourly Demand and Forecast Data and Their Effect on Forecast Evaluation and Annual Peaks

The U.S. Form EIA-930 files give hourly demand and day-ahead forecasts for each Lower-48 balancing authority (BA) and are widely used to evaluate load forecasts. With a pre-specified plan we screened 53 BAs over 2016 to 2026 (4.99 million BA-hours) for non-positive values, gross outliers, spikes, flat runs, copied forecasts and timestamp offsets. We compared the screen with EIA's adjusted demand and measured the errors' effect on each BA's annual forecast MAPE and annual peak. Errors are rare: the median BA has 0.022% anomalous demand hours and 0.026% anomalous forecast hours, and the forecast's rate exceeded demand's in only 23 of 47 BAs. Their effects are concentrated but not small. Screening changed a BA's annual MAPE by more than 10% in 15.8% of BA-years, and by more than half in 42. The annual peak from reported demand exceeded EIA's adjusted peak by more than 1% in 17.5% of BA-years, usually by more than a factor of two. Nearly every value EIA adjusts is one our screen flags. But EIA leaves most flagged values unchanged, including all flat runs and all but 5% of spikes, and it does not adjust the forecast series at all. An exploratory analysis found that since February 2019 PJM's forecast has been stamped one hour early relative to its demand in every month. Realigning it lowers its MAPE from 3.6% to 2.4%, the pre-2019 level. CISO, AZPS and SC show shorter offsets. We recommend screening both series and checking their alignment before any evaluation.

Keywords: Data quality, load forecasting, forecast evaluation, peak demand, EIA-930.
