Recording Errors in EIA-930 Hourly Demand and Forecast Data and Their Effect on Forecast Evaluation and Annual Peaks

The U.S. Form EIA-930 files give hourly demand and day-ahead forecasts for each Lower-48 balancing authority (BA) and are widely used to evaluate load forecasts. Earlier cleaning of these files targets demand alone, and the effect of errors on forecast evaluation has not, to our knowledge, been measured across BAs. Under a pre-specified plan we screened both series of 53 BAs in 2016–2026 (4.99 million BA-hours) for non-positive values, gross outliers, spikes, flat runs, copied forecasts and timestamp offsets, compared the screen with EIA's adjusted demand by class, and measured their effect on each BA's annual forecast MAPE and peak. Errors are rare: the median BA has 0.022% anomalous demand and 0.026% anomalous forecast hours, and the forecast's rate exceeded demand's in only 23 of 47 BAs. Their effects are concentrated but not small. Screening changed a BA's annual MAPE by more than 10% in 15.8% of BA-years, and by more than half in 42. The reported annual peak differed from EIA's adjusted peak by more than 1% in 17.5% of BA-years, usually because it was over twice as high. EIA's adjustments are nearly a subset of our flags but leave all flat runs and all but 5% of spikes unchanged, and never touch the forecast. An exploratory monthly scan confirms a recently reported one-hour offset in PJM's forecast since February 2019; realigning it lowers PJM's MAPE from 3.6% to 2.4%. CISO, AZPS and SC show shorter offsets. Both series should be screened and aligned before any evaluation.

Keywords: Data quality, load forecasting, forecast evaluation, peak demand, EIA-930.
